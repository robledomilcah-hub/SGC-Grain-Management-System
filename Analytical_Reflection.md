# Analytical Reflection

Working with an AI assistant helped me understand the business needs of Sintang Grains Cooperative before creating the prototype. At first, the AI could suggest many possible entities and features, such as warehouse management, suppliers, truck schedules, payments, and detailed financial records. However, these features were not directly mentioned in the stakeholder interviews. Adding them would make the system more complicated and could distract from the actual problems of the cooperative. I refined the scope to focus on Farmer, Grain, Delivery, Buyer, and Order because these entities directly support the needs described by the manager, records clerk, and regional grain trader.

One important correction was the delivery form. A complicated form with many required farmer details would slow down the records clerk, especially during busy harvest periods. I therefore kept the form simple: farmer ID or name, grain type, quantity in kilograms, and delivery date. Another important business rule was inventory validation. The system must add stock when a delivery is recorded and subtract stock only when an order is confirmed. It must also reject an order when the requested quantity is greater than the available stock. This helps prevent the cooperative from promising grain that it does not have.

Collaborating with AI on schema design was different from drawing static UML or ERD diagrams by hand. When drawing manually, I need to think through the entities and relationships on my own before producing the diagram. With AI, I could ask questions, request explanations, challenge suggestions, and revise the model step by step. AI made brainstorming faster, but its suggestions still needed to be checked against the actual interview statements. I learned that AI should be treated as a collaborator, not as the final decision-maker.

Overall, the activity showed me that a useful system does not need many features. It should solve the stakeholders' real problems in a clear and simple way. The prototype demonstrates faster delivery recording, updated inventory, safer buyer orders, printable confirmations, and a basic report for management.
